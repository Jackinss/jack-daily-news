Marketing Dive, NRN · November 5, 2026

McDonald's loses its US marketing chief after a sales dip, following a quarter the company itself called underwhelming for domestic marketing

Alyssa Buetikofer is leaving McDonald's after 15 years as US chief marketing and commercial officer, staying through December 18 to hand off, "to spend more time with family." Global CMO Morgan Flatley and SVP Charlie Newberger step in as interim leaders while McDonald's searches for a replacement. The announcement landed a day after McDonald's reported Q3 2026 US comparable sales down 0.2%, even as global comparable sales rose 1.5% and the company beat Wall Street's overall estimates. The shakeup follows a spring and summer of domestic marketing moves the company has acknowledged fell short, including the $3-and-under value push, and this fall's decision (issue 20) to end a 32-year FIFA World Cup sponsorship because the borrowed moment had stopped paying for itself.

Why it mattersA US-only miss inside an otherwise-beating quarter is an odd trigger for a 15-year marketing chief to leave, unless the real problem was never the number but what she was asked to sell with it.
tecnoandroid.it, corrierecomunicazioni.it · November 5-6, 2026

Fastweb puts an actual date on retiring the Vodafone name in Italy: a relaunch in 2027, a hard deadline in 2029

Fastweb confirmed on November 5 that its first nine months of 2026 are on track, with cost synergies from the Vodafone Italia merger, completed January 1, 2026 after Swisscom's acquisition, running ahead of the over-300-million-euro 2026 target (issue 24). A day later, reporting filled in the next step: Fastweb plans to relaunch under a restyled version of its own historic yellow logo, not an entirely new name, in the first half of 2027. The Vodafone brand cannot be used at all, not even inside the "Fastweb + Vodafone" corporate name, once its licensing deal expires in 2029. Fastweb filed three new logo designs with the Italian patent office back in March.

Why it mattersWhen two famous telecom names merge, the slower, older brand usually wins by default, not by vote, the same arithmetic Kimberly-Clark is still working out with Kenvue (issue 22).
Resemble AI, via PPC Land · changes effective Oct. 28 and Nov. 12, reported Oct. 28, 2026

Meta turns on two features that inflate reported sales right before Black Friday, the same month its own ad library turns up thousands of undisclosed AI ads

Meta switched on in-app product browsing by default for existing Facebook and Instagram ads on October 28, keeping shoppers inside the app instead of sending them to the advertiser's own site; Meta claims this alone delivers "3% more conversions on average." From November 12, Meta also started counting purchases by matching device-side data by default, not only server-side, a change that can make reported conversions jump with no real change in campaign performance, right as advertisers start reconciling their Black Friday numbers. In the same window, AI-detection firm Resemble AI scanned 37,768 Meta ad files from September 2026 and flagged 3,379 of them, 8.9%, as AI-generated or manipulated; only two of those flagged files, 0.06%, mentioned AI anywhere in the ad copy. The split by page type was stark: 6.9% of ads on real brand pages were flagged, versus 71.3% on known scam or arbitrage "bait" pages.

Why it mattersThree changes, one direction: advertisers are about to see bigger numbers on their dashboards this Black Friday for reasons that have nothing to do with better creative, better targeting, or more honest disclosure.
PPC Land, media-buyer interviews · November 2-3, 2026

Google moves AI Overviews for brand searches into the side column, and buyers say up to 30% of search budgets are now in question

On November 2, Google relocated AI Overviews for branded keyword searches from the main results column to the right-hand rail on desktop, replacing the knowledge panels brands used to control with AI-written summaries they cannot edit. AI Overviews now appear on 82.91% of branded keyword results as of late September, and 63 of 100 tracked brand names displayed them by the same date. Media buyers quoted by PPC Land put real numbers on the resulting anxiety: Farhad Divecha of Accuracast said about 10% of client budgets are already under question, rising to 20-30% during the holidays; Bryce Hamlin of Power Digital said 10-30% of search budgets are being reallocated elsewhere. The concern lands as Google's own Q3 search ad revenue grew just 12%, its slowest pace of 2026.

Why it mattersWhen the thing a brand spent years ranking for gets rewritten by an AI summary it cannot touch, the question stops being "how do we rank" and becomes "do we still need to pay for this traffic at all."

Social trends

Contentgrip, eMarketer, PhocusWire · November 10, 2026

The ad becomes a conversation: Priceline's Penny is the first AI agent to sit inside a Snapchat ad

  • Priceline is the first travel brand to use Snapchat's new AI Sponsored Snaps format, placing its own AI assistant, Penny, directly inside the paid ad unit.
  • Instead of clicking straight through, users can ask Penny about destinations and draft itineraries inside the Snap; any dates or preferences they share carry over automatically when they continue to Priceline's app to book.
  • Penny runs on Anthropic's Claude models, integrated since June 2026. Priceline says the assistant converts at roughly twice the rate of users who skip it, though that figure is Priceline's own, not independently audited.
Why it mattersFor a century the best salesperson was the one who got the customer talking before ever asking for the sale; Snapchat and Priceline just rebuilt that inside a paid ad unit, with the customer's own words doing the targeting.
Mintegral / Insightrackr, via Contentgrip · report published August 25, 2026, covering H1 2026

Microdrama went from niche format to a 700-advertiser-a-month channel, and this week's Welch's launch is the live proof

  • Downloads of microdrama apps, the bite-sized vertical dramas built for daily repeat viewing, grew 95.5% year over year in H1 2026, to 1.45 billion.
  • Active advertisers on these apps grew 132% in the same period, and the creative volume they produced grew 151%, with more than 700 companies advertising on short-drama apps every month by June.
  • The report's own warning: "more advertisers can also mean faster creative fatigue."
Why it mattersA format this cheap to scale and this early in its advertiser curve is exactly the kind of channel that looks undervalued for about six months, then looks crowded for the next five years; Welch's (Campaigns, below) is moving in while it's still the former.

What is working in content

Resemble AI, IAB + Sonata Insights

Audiences don't punish AI ads, they punish AI ads that pretend not to be AI ads

This week's Resemble AI scan (The world, above) found Meta's own ad library is already 8.9% AI-made or altered with only 0.06% disclosure, and the split between real brand pages (6.9% flagged) and scam "bait" pages (71.3% flagged) is the tell: the advertisers getting caught hiding AI are disproportionately the ones with the least to lose from getting caught. That lines up with the most recent large study on the question: IAB and Sonata Insights surveyed 505 Gen Z and millennial consumers and 104 ad executives (fielded October 2025 to January 2026, published mid-January 2026) and found only 45% of consumers feel positive about AI-generated ads versus 82% of executives who assume they do, a 37-point gap that widened from 32 points in a similar 2024 survey. The same study found consumers call AI ads "manipulative" (20% vs. 10% of executives) and "unethical" (16% vs. 7%) at roughly double the executive rate.

Why it mattersThe data has said the same thing for almost a year now; the problem was never the AI, it's brands hoping nobody notices.
McDonald's, Target corporate newsroom

Fifteen years versus two months: what a marketing chief's clock looks like on either side of a sales miss

The same week McDonald's lost a 15-year marketing chief over a quarterly sales miss (The world, above), Target's marketing turnaround was two months old. Target named Mark Weinstein, previously Hilton's global CMO across all 28 of its brands, as chief marketing and guest experience officer on September 14, 2026. He also now runs Roundel, Target's retail media network, and Target+, its marketplace, folding brand marketing and the commerce engine under one person inside CEO Michael Fiddelke's $6 billion transformation plan: cleaner, less cluttered stores, faster pickup, and 75% of the home decor line turned over by June, by Target's own figures.

Why it mattersWhen a retailer is this far underwater, the marketing chief increasingly gets both jobs, fix the brand and run the commerce engine, because there's no longer room to keep them separate; it's the same "fast and lean" instinct Kimberly-Clark used for its own combined CMO role this month (issue 24).

The historical bridge

Hartford, Connecticut, 1906

The $75 brush salesman who built an empire on getting the customer talking first

In 1906, Alfred C. Fuller started the Fuller Brush Company with a $75 investment, making brushes in his sister's basement and selling them himself, door to door, around Hartford. His whole method rested on one fact: a salesman who got inside a home and started a real conversation beat any printed advertisement, letting the homeowner handle the brush, answer a question, talk about her own cleaning problems, before he ever asked for the sale. Fuller credited his success to "farm-boy charm" and a full money-back guarantee; by the company's own account, two out of every three homes where the "Fuller Brush Man" was allowed to make his full pitch ended in an order worth three to seven dollars. By 1919, thirteen years in, the company was doing more than a million dollars a year, and its door-to-door model went on to shape Tupperware, Avon and Amway.

Why it still holds. Priceline's Penny (Social trends, above) does exactly what the Fuller Brush Man did: get the customer talking about their own trip, their own dates, their own preferences, before the sale is even offered. It just happens inside a Snapchat ad instead of on a porch, 120 years later.
